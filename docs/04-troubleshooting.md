# Troubleshooting

*[Nederlands](04-troubleshooting.nl.md) · [Deutsch](04-troubleshooting.de.md)*

| What you see | Why | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **"Collecting data"** | The battery has not gone from 95 % or higher down to 15 % or lower since monitoring started. In autumn and winter that can take a while. | Nothing is wrong. For a quick first measurement, let the battery charge fully once and then run down completely. |
| **The battery charged full and ran down completely, but there is no measurement yet** | The monitor closes a discharge only once the state of charge **rises 3 points** from its lowest point (for example from 11 to 14 %). That way a short pause or a small dip does not end the measurement too early. While the battery sits empty, the card shows "Full discharge running". | Nothing; the measurement appears as soon as the battery starts charging again. A restart in the meantime does not cost the measurement, as long as the battery is idle. |
| **No measurement, although the battery often runs far down** | A full discharge must go in one go from 95 % or more to 15 % or less. If the battery charges back up by **3 points or more** on the way (sun, an automation of your own, an EMS or charge control), the swing ends there and does not count as a full discharge. | Now and then let the battery run down in one go: pause automations that charge in between. During a full discharge the card shows "Full discharge running" with the starting percentage. |
| **Cards are in English (or German) while you want another language** | The cards follow the language of your Home Assistant **profile**, not the system language. | Set the language in your profile, or add `lang: nl`, `lang: de` or `lang: en` to the card code. |
| **The scan finds no battery** | The scan looks for a state-of-charge sensor (%) and a power sensor with "battery power" in its name, on the same device or with the same name prefix. Some integrations use other names. | Choose **Choose sensors manually** and pick the two sensors yourself. |
| **Status "No recent data"** | The battery was charging or discharging (more than 50 W), but the state of charge and the power reported no new value for an hour. | Check the battery connection (ESPHome device, Modbus bridge, MQTT). The status returns by itself once data comes in. |
| **Status "Sensor unavailable"** | The state of charge or power sensor is unavailable; the card names it. | Check that integration and the entity. If the entity was renamed, pick it again under **Configure** or add the battery again. |
| **Cell spread stays empty** | A sample needs a full charge (≥ 95 %) and 5 minutes at rest. The next sample needs the battery to drop below 90 % first. | Nothing; it follows after the next full charge. Check that both cell sensors show a voltage (V or mV). |
| **Warning: "looks like the AC / grid side"** | The chosen power sensor has "ac", "grid", "net", "p1" or "meter" in its name. AC values include conversion losses and give a wrong capacity. | Pick the battery (DC) power sensor. Only if you are sure it is the DC side: submit again to use it anyway. |
| **Notification: "plus and minus reversed automatically"** | Your power sensor shows charging as a negative value. The monitor noticed this on the first full charge or discharge and corrected it. | Nothing. If detection got it wrong, set the sign manually under **Configure**. |
| **A measurement seems missing after a restart** | A Home Assistant restart during a discharge is bridged when it takes at most 10 minutes. If it takes longer, or there was no power value just before the restart, that swing is not used. | Nothing; the next full discharge counts again. |
| **Percentage above 100 % (card: "provisional")** | Higher than new is hardly possible for a battery. Usually it comes from the **battery's SOC meter**. The monitor divides the energy that came out by the number of percent the state of charge dropped. If that percentage stands still for a while at the top or the bottom while energy is still coming out, every percent seems to hold more energy. Example: a Venus E went from 12 to 11 % while about 5 percent worth of energy still came out. The internal reserve (depth of discharge) you set plays **no** part here: it only changes the kWh, not the percentage. | Nothing: after 3 measurements the card shows the middle value, and the chart shows such a measurement as an open dot (○) once the monitor sees it. If it stays above 100 %, charge the battery to full once in one go, until charging really stops: that puts the SOC meter right (see the SOC panel). If it is still off after that, report it in a GitHub issue. |
| **About half (card: "Check the model")** | The measurement comes out at 40–60 % of new. A battery that really lost half is rare; usually the **wrong model was picked**. A Venus C has 2.56 kWh, half a Venus E, and can report the same model code. | Is it a Venus C? Under **Configure**, set the rated capacity to 2.56 kWh and the reserve to 0.26 kWh; the measurements are recalculated. Is the model right? Then report it via the Marstek app. |
| **Statistics graph on the standard card is empty** | Home Assistant builds long-term statistics per hour; monthly averages appear after the first measurements. | Wait a few days; the overview card shows values right away. |
| **A card shows a red "sensor.marstek_… ?" or "Sensor not found"** | The battery code in the card does not match the code you chose (e.g. `mt1`). | Use the same code in the card as when adding the battery, or leave `battery:` out to take the first battery. |
| **"Custom element doesn't exist: marstek-capacity-card" or the dashboard choice is missing** | The cards are loaded by the integration. That only happens after a restart, with at least one battery added. | Add a battery, restart Home Assistant and refresh the browser (Ctrl+F5). The one-click dashboard needs Home Assistant 2026.5 or newer. |
| **Battery capacity is not in the sidebar** (from 0.5.1) | The menu item appears only once at least one battery is added and Home Assistant has restarted. It is off when **Menu item in the sidebar** is off for one of the batteries. You may also have hidden it yourself. If you already have a dashboard of your own at `/marstek-capacity`, the monitor leaves it alone. | Check under **Configure** of each battery that the menu item is on, restart and refresh the browser (Ctrl+F5). Hidden? Long-press **Home Assistant** at the top of the sidebar (or go to your profile → change the sidebar) and bring the item back. This is per device. |
| **Nothing of this integration under Settings → Dashboards → Resources** | That is correct. The integration loads its own cards itself, on every page of Home Assistant. No resource is needed. | Nothing; do not add a resource. |
| **"Error loading the dashboard strategy: Timeout waiting for strategy element ll-strategy-dashboard-marstek-capacity to be registered"** | Up to 0.5.0 the card file could be ready before Home Assistant itself. The dashboard then did not see the cards. Mostly in Firefox and on Android. | Update to 0.5.1 or newer, restart Home Assistant and refresh the browser (Ctrl+F5). |

Removing a battery from the integration keeps its measurements in `.storage/`, so they can still support a report to Marstek. To start over on purpose, use the action `marstek_capacity_monitor.reset_measurements`.
